PROCEDURE: Confined Space Entry and Permit
Document ID: SOP-CONF-01
Applies to: tanks, vessels, pits and any enclosed space with limited entry and exit, including looking or reaching inside to work on a valve or fitting.
DEMO CONTENT: fictional text for a demonstration. It is not real safety or engineering guidance.

WHEN TO USE THIS PROCEDURE
Use this procedure when a job needs a person to enter, or to put their head or upper body into, a tank, vessel or other enclosed space. Typical requests: "check the valve inside tank 12", "look inside the vessel", "clean the sump". Assets with hazard class confined_space always need this procedure.

STOP - PERMIT REQUIRED
Entry is not allowed without a valid confined-space entry permit signed by the permit issuer. The technician must not start, and the assistant must not draft a work log for the entry, until the technician confirms that a permit is in place.

PERMIT REQUIREMENTS
1. Isolate the space from all inflows and energy sources (lockout/tagout, SOP-LOTO-01), including blanking or locking process valves.
2. Empty, vent and clean the space as required.
3. Test the atmosphere (oxygen, flammable gas, toxic gas) before entry and at intervals; record the readings on the permit.
4. Assign a standby attendant outside the space who stays in contact and does not enter.
5. Confirm a rescue plan and rescue equipment are ready.
6. Entrants wear the required protective equipment and are logged in and out.

IF THE JOB DOES NOT NEED ENTRY
Work on the outside of the tank (external inspection, valve operation from outside) does not need the entry permit, but still needs lockout/tagout when energy or pressure is present.

AFTER THE JOB
Count everyone out, close the permit, remove locks (SOP-LOTO-01), and record the work in the work log (POL-WORKLOG-01), including the permit number.
