GUIDE: Post-Incident Notes and Review
Runbook ID: RB-POST-01
Applies to: every incident after service is restored or the alert is acknowledged as noise.

WHEN TO WRITE POST-INCIDENT NOTES
- Within one business day for critical and major incidents.
- A short note is enough for minor incidents and for alerts that turned out to be test noise.

WHAT TO RECORD
1. Summary: one or two sentences describing what users or systems experienced.
2. Timeline in UTC: first symptom, alert fired, diagnosis steps, approval requested, remediation executed, service verified.
3. Root cause: the single cause, stated with the evidence that proves it (log lines, metric values, deployment record, certificate expiry).
4. What was ruled out: other suspects that were checked and why they were excluded (for example a recent deployment to an unrelated healthy service).
5. Remediation: the action name, target service, parameters, who approved it, and the verification result.
6. Impact: affected service, duration, error rate peak, customer impact.
7. Follow-up actions with an owner and a due date.

TYPICAL FOLLOW-UPS BY CAUSE
- Bad deployment: add a performance check to the release pipeline, review slow query and N+1 patterns before release, require a canary stage.
- Connection pool exhaustion: review pool sizing against traffic, look for connection leaks, add an alert at 80% pool utilization.
- Expired certificate: add expiry alerts at 30 and 7 days, keep a certificate inventory with owners, automate renewal.
- Scheduled load test noise: annotate or suppress alerts during planned test windows, and announce tests in advance.

STYLE
Blameless. Describe systems and decisions, not people. Be specific with numbers and times. Keep it short enough to be read by someone who was not there.
