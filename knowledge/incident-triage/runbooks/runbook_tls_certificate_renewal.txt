RUNBOOK: Expired TLS Certificate - Renewal and Rotation
Runbook ID: RB-TLS-01
Applies to: gateways and ingress endpoints that terminate TLS. The supported remediation action is "certificate_rotation" and it is available for the auth-gateway service.

WHEN TO USE THIS RUNBOOK
Use this runbook when clients cannot complete a secure connection and users report login or authentication failures, while the service process itself is running. Typical symptoms:
- A large share of requests fail at the TLS handshake (for example 25% to 50% of attempts) while the rest succeed.
- Log lines such as "SSLHandshakeException", "TLS handshake rejected", "Certificate Expired", "CERT_DATE_INVALID", or "Ingress router rejecting HTTPS connections due to invalid/expired TLS certificate".
- A certificate warning in the logs that names a domain and an expiry timestamp in the past.
- Service health shows a TLS certificate with status EXPIRED and a negative days-until-expiration value.
- Existing sessions or tokens keep working; only new connections or new logins fail.
- Throughput drops because connections are rejected at the ingress, while CPU is not high.

DIAGNOSIS STEPS
1. Check service health for the affected service and read its tls_certificate block: domain, status, expires_at and days_until_expiration.
2. Query ERROR logs and confirm that handshake failures name an expired certificate rather than a protocol or cipher mismatch.
3. Check the error rate and throughput metrics to size the impact.
4. Check whether a recent deployment touched the service. A certificate that expired on a date before the problem began is the cause, even if no deployment happened.
5. Do not restart as a first step. A restart reloads the same expired certificate and does not fix the problem.

DECISION
If the certificate status is EXPIRED (or the expiry timestamp is in the past) and handshake errors cite certificate expiry, the root cause is an expired certificate.

REMEDIATION
- Action name: certificate_rotation
- Target service: the service whose certificate expired (for example auth-gateway)
- Parameter: domain = the certificate domain shown in the health data (for example a wildcard internal domain).
- certificate_rotation requires human approval. Propose it through the remediation approval flow with a justification that cites the domain, the expiry timestamp, and the handshake errors.
- Rotation issues a new certificate and reloads it on the ingress without a full outage.

VERIFICATION AFTER ROTATION
- Certificate status is VALID with a new expiry date in the future.
- TLS handshakes succeed and the authentication error rate returns to baseline.
- Throughput recovers toward its normal level.

PREVENTION
Add an alert that fires at least 30 days before any certificate expires, and list every certificate with its owner. Record the follow-up in the post-incident notes.
